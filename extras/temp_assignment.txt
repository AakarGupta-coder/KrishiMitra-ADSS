1.  Purpose of the Assignment
Read and critically compare recent research papers rather than merely summarize them.
Convert a broad application area into a specific, testable problem statement.
Design and implement a system, and benchmark it against published baselines.
Communicate technical work in IEEE conference format or in a patent disclosure format. (Publishing is not mandatory but document submission is mandatory).

4.  DA1 — Problem Identification, Literature Survey, Problem Statement and Proposed Architecture
Weightage: 10 marks     |     Suggested length: 4–5 pages
4.1  What to Submit
(a)  Problem Identification — Domain and Motivation
Describe the application domain and why it matters. Quantify it using credible sources (WHO, government reports, peer-reviewed surveys) rather than vague claims.
Identify the stakeholder and the decision your system supports.
(b)  Literature Survey
Minimum 15 papers, of which at least 10 must be from the last three years and from reputed venues (IEEE, Elsevier, Springer, MICCAI, CVPR, NeurIPS, ACL, or Q1/Q2 journals).
Conference papers and unreviewed preprints may be cited as supporting material only, and must not exceed three references.
Present the survey as a comparison table, not as paragraph-wise summaries:


Follow the table with a Research Gap subsection listing 3–5 gaps that emerge from it. 
(c)  Problem Statement
A single well-formed paragraph containing four elements:

Weak: “We will use deep learning for brain tumour detection.”
Acceptable: “Given multi-parametric MRI volumes in which one or more sequences may be missing at inference time, segment the three tumour sub-regions such that mean Dice degrades by less than 5% relative to a full-modality nnU-Net baseline, evaluated across all 15 modality subsets of BraTS 2021.”
(d)  Proposed System Architecture
A block diagram of the complete pipeline: data acquisition → preprocessing → model → post-processing → evaluation → output/interface.
A detailed model diagram with layer/module names, input–output tensor shapes, and the location of your proposed novelty clearly highlighted.
Justification for each design choice, backed by a citation or a stated hypothesis. “We added attention because it improves performance” is not a justification.
Planned experimental setup: dataset, split strategy, evaluation metrics, baselines, hardware, and the ablation studies you intend to run.
(e)  Feasibility Note
Half a page covering compute available, dataset size and access status, estimated training time, and identified risks with a fallback plan.
